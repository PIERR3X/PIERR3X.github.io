MSc Thesis Internship | CFD Analyst @ R&D CFD SRL, in collaboration with DUMAREY group · Modena · 11/2025 – 05/2026

CFD of a high-pressure hydrogen injector

Analysis of CFD methodologies applied to a high-pressure H2 injector in support of the design process. Supervisor: Stefano Fontanesi.

Context
Master’s thesis internship as CFD Analyst at R&D CFD SRL, in collaboration with DUMAREY group
Tools
STAR-CCM+, experimental data correlation, Excel, PowerPoint
Methods
Steady and unsteady CFD, segregated and coupled solvers, mesh motion (Morphing, Overset), fluid-structure interaction (DFBI)

The problem

A high-pressure injector has moving parts and fast transients, so one steady simulation may not be enough. A design team needs to know which modelling level is sufficient and which one is worth its computing cost.

The work

I modelled the injector in STAR-CCM+ and compared approaches of growing complexity: steady and unsteady simulations, without and with mesh motion (morphing and overset techniques), and models that take into account the fluid-structure interaction and the external actions to compute the body motion (DFBI approach), using experimental data for correlation and model validation.

The result

The thesis analyses these CFD methodologies as a support to the injector design process, weighing what each one can predict against its cost. A steady-state model predicts mass flow rate as well as the transient one and is the right tool for fast design evaluation. A 2D DFBI model with overset mesh predicts the needle motion from forces alone and reproduces the sealing contacts at more than 50% lower cost than morphing, which makes it a reliable tool to validate concepts. The morphing technique remains the most reliable for final design analysis and validation.

The degree was awarded with 108/110.

Aerodynamics workshop, Advanced Automotive Engineering · December 2023 · team of 8

Sidepod inlet blanking on a Formula 3 car

Reduce radiator cooling by 3% in the most efficient way, while keeping the aerodynamic balance at 47%.

Context
CFD workshop followed by a wind tunnel workshop, baseline 2017 Formula 3 car
Tools
CFD post-processing, wind tunnel polars and aeromaps
Target
Cooling −3.0% (±0.2%), front balance 47% (±0.4%)

The problem

Closing part of the sidepod inlet cuts the flow through the radiator, which can trade cooling for efficiency. The team had to find which blanking option gets to −3% cooling with the smallest efficiency loss, then restore the 47% balance using the front flap polar.

The work

The first part of the work was the analysis of CFD results for a bottom and an internal blanking, to identify the options to test in the wind tunnel that came closest to the project targets. In the wind tunnel we built a run list, changed it live when the first run disagreed with CFD, and tested 20% and 35% bottom, 35% external and 35% internal blanking. Then we tried removing the pod wing, set the front wing at 20° to rebalance the car, and fine-tuned the cooling with strips of tape.

The result

The final setup is a 35% external blanking with 15 mm of extra tape, a front wing angle of 20° and the pod wing mounted. It reached −2.98% radiator flow at 47% balance, against a target of −3.0%. Removing the pod wing reduced cooling a little but worsened efficiency, so it was put back. The final configuration also has the lowest balance sensitivity at small roll angles.

Wind tunnel and CFD disagreed on the first run, which is why the run list was reordered during the session.

Dynamic testing of vehicles · A.Y. 2023/24 · team of 9

Modal testing of a BMW LMP2 rear wing support

Measure the natural frequencies of a carbon component and check them against a finite element model.

Context
Teamwork project of the vehicle dynamic testing course, with Dallara criteria
Tools
Impact hammer, acquisition system, FFT and modal software, Ansys modal analysis
Setup
Free-free condition, bandwidth 1–1600 Hz

The problem

A rear wing support sees engine, gearbox, wing flutter and track irregularities. Dallara does not accept a component whose first natural frequency is below 15 Hz, so the stiffness of the real part has to be proven, not assumed.

The work

We chose the driving points, calibrated the setup, ran impact tests on the suspended component and checked the coherence of every measurement. In parallel we built an Ansys model from the CAD, with assumed carbon material properties, and compared mode shapes and frequencies. Finally we clamped the model on its real mounting holes.

The result

The model and the experiment agree on the mode shapes and, for the first three modes, on the frequencies. With the real constraints the first natural frequency is 98 Hz, far above the 15 Hz limit, so the component is validated. Damping is between 0.1% and 0.5%, higher than the metal parts tested by the other groups (0.27% against 0.03%), as expected for a carbon skin.

Design of racing car composite structures · A.Y. 2023/24 · team of 5

Composites laboratory

Thermal, mechanical and bonding tests on the materials used for racing car structures.

Context
Laboratory report of the composite structures course
Tests
DSC, DMA, tension, compression, single lap joint shear
Materials
Carbon-epoxy T800/ER450, T300/ER434 and M46J/ER434, sand-blasted aluminium joints

The problem

The properties on a datasheet depend on the cure, the lay-up and the way a part is tested. Before using a composite in a car, someone has to measure them with the right standard and understand the scatter.

The work

We ran differential scanning calorimetry on uncured and cured samples to find the glass transition and the degree of cure, dynamic mechanical analysis for the viscoelastic behaviour, tensile and compression tests with their standards, and single lap shear tests on aluminium plates bonded at three adhesive thicknesses (0.25, 1 and 2.5 mm). Each result was compared with the 2022 edition of the lab.

The result

181.7 °Cglass transition of T800/ER450, in line with the datasheet
27 MPaaverage adhesive shear strength with a 0.25 mm bond line

The thermal tests confirmed a complete cure: the second heating ramp showed no residual exothermic reaction. T800/ER450 reached a Tg of 181.7 °C, and a shoulder in its thermogram points to a second resin constituent reacting at higher temperature; M46J/ER434 stopped at 121.8 °C, in line with its lower service temperature. DMA showed the trade-off between the two systems: T300/ER434 had the higher storage modulus (110.7 against 87.5 GPa), T800/ER450 the higher glass transition (139 against 109 °C).

In tension at 0° T800/ER450 failed at 1176 MPa, about twice T300/ER434 (560 MPa), while the toughened ER434 resin gave more elongation at 45°, so more absorbed energy. In compression many 0° coupons failed under the tabs, which pointed to a tab bonding issue rather than to the material. The lap joints kept about 27 MPa of shear strength at every bond line: a thicker adhesive moved the failure from mixed to mainly adhesive and made the joint more plastic and less stiff, but not weaker. All values were consistent with the supplier’s data, even though it was obtained with a different standard.

Composite structures, Abaqus activity · February 2024 · team of 5

Composite structures in Abaqus

Failure of unidirectional coupons, sandwich panel modelling and adhesive debonding with a cohesive zone.

Context
Three homework assignments of the course, presented to the class
Tools
Abaqus/CAE (shell, solid, cohesive elements), ply calculator
Material
AS4/3501-6 carbon-epoxy, aluminium skins, CFRP skin and stiffener

The problem

A first design needs a fast tool, but the fast tool has to be trusted. The questions were how far a ply calculator is from a finite element model, which failure criterion is safer, and how much the modelling choice changes a sandwich panel result.

The work

Homework 1. A 508 × 102 × 3.81 mm unidirectional coupon, loaded axially for ply angles from 0° to 90°, with failure load found at failure index 1 for the maximum stress theory and the Tsai-Wu criterion, including the effect of the cross term F12 and then angle-ply laminates. Homework 2. A 2 × 2 m sandwich panel with 1 mm aluminium skins and a 30 mm core, under 500 Pa, modelled with laminate shells, with solid core and solid shells, and with solid core and thin skins. Homework 3. Debonding of a skin-stiffener joint under tension, with three adhesive spew fillet shapes and a cohesive zone model.

Table 2 Sandwich panel, maximum skin stress and deflection
ModelStress (MPa)Displacement (mm)
Laminate shells3.5851.730
Solid core, solid shells3.3341.755
Solid core, thin skins3.2911.777
Handbook theory2.8901.504

The result

The ply calculator stays within 7% of Abaqus for the maximum stress theory and 3% for Tsai-Wu, so it is a reliable first design tool and it is conservative on the failure load. Tsai-Wu is the more conservative criterion in tension: the maximum stress theory predicts up to 31% more load at 30°. The cross term F12 moves the Tsai-Wu load by up to about 10%. The three sandwich models agree within 9% on stress and 3% on displacement, while the handbook formula is only a rough estimate. In the debonding study the separation starts at 1875 N for case A and 2699 N for case C, so the fillet shape changes the strength of the joint.

Automotive Computer Aided Design · MUNER · team of 4

Magnetorheological suspension for Pikes Peak

From competitor analysis to manufacturing process, in a product design report of about 90 pages.

Context
Group project of the Advanced Automotive Engineering course
Tools
Quality Function Deployment, function structures, Simulink, CAD and technical drawings
Product
Adaptive damper for an off-road hill climb car

The problem

The Pikes Peak hill climb is 19.99 km long with more than 155 corners, on a mountain road without barriers and with changing weather. A damper that adapts its stiffness in real time lets the driver push harder while keeping the tyres on the road.

The work

We started from the application and the competitors, rated them on performance, reliability and price, and wrote the requirements. A House of Quality linked customer wishes to technical targets. We then estimated costs and the break-even between production options, for a volume of at most 1000 units. The conceptual design went from an abstraction of the fluid-piston interaction to function structures and working principles: check valves with shim packs for bump and rebound, a spool valve, the position of the coil, monotube against twin tube. The last step chose the manufacturing process.

The result

The report ends with a defined damper concept, drawings, and machining chosen over extrusion for the part: the geometry is simple, the mould of extrusion is expensive for such a small volume, and machining gives the same or better precision at a lower cost.

Mechanical vibrations, MATLAB teamwork · A.Y. 2022/23 · team of 7

Vibration of an Alfa Romeo V6 engine on its mounts

How much the engine shakes and what force reaches the chassis, from standstill to 6500 rpm.

My role
Team leader; numerical implementation, with one teammate
Tools
MATLAB (symbolic, eig, ode45, FFT), SolidWorks for inertias
Engine
Alfa Romeo Busso 3.2 V6 24V, 60°, firing order 1-4-3-6-2-5

The problem

A V6 never has a perfect balance. The unbalanced torques excite roll, pitch and yaw of the engine on its four elastic mounts, and the forces go into the chassis. The question was where the resonances are and whether they matter.

The work

The engine is a rigid body with six degrees of freedom on four triaxial springs and dampers, with Rayleigh damping. The numerical implementation, which I did with a teammate, builds the inertial forces of the six pistons with the exact crank equation, solves the equations of motion by modal analysis and checks the answer with a second integration in ode45. It also runs run-up and run-down ramps, 100 steady speeds and the spectra of displacement and transmitted forces, and verifies Parseval’s theorem.

The result

The forces are balanced overall, while the torques are the main source of vibration. At low speed the engine crosses its resonances with a peak of about 30 mm, but only for an instant and outside the working range. Above that the response is stable, so the engine is at its best at high speed. The modal solution and the ode45 integration agree.
